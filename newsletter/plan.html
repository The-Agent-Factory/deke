Favicon

The site still shows the default Next.js icon that came with the starter kit. The logo on the site is just the words "Deke Sharon" in navy and gold, so the icon should use the same two colours. Three concepts, each shown at real sizes. The 16px one is what people see in a browser tab.

Three stacked notes (a chord, i.e. voices in harmony) sit inside the D. At tab size they drop out and it reads as a clean D.

What ships once you pick one

  • The full set: tab icon (16, 32, 48), a sharp SVG for modern browsers, the 180px icon for iPhone home screens, and 192/512 for Android. Next.js picks them up from src/app/ automatically.
  • The same mark goes in the newsletter header, so the email and the site look like one brand.
  • Deploy: committed to main, railway up from a clean worktree, then I check that dekesharon.com/favicon.ico has actually changed. (Today it is the 25,931-byte default.)

One Voice, the weekly newsletter

This is the advice I'd give as Deke's newsletter editor, followed by how I would run it every week. The brief is to help singers and a cappella directors, and to point them gently toward Total Vocal on Skool. Deke spends about 10 minutes a week on it, on his phone.

The expert calls

CallWhy
Weekly, short, one ideaThe March framework planned a long monthly essay. A weekly email works when it is a 3-minute read with one thing to try at rehearsal. Each week it expands the theme the social posts already cover, so we never need a separate idea.
Keep the name "One Voice"That is the name readers saw on dekesharon.com/news when they signed up. The March draft used "The Arrangement". Pick one; I recommend the one already live.
Only Deke's real wordsEvery story and tip comes from something Deke has said on record: YouTube, Skool lives, his posts. The review page shows the source and timestamp next to each paragraph. We never make up an anecdote in his name.
90% help, 10% SkoolOne Skool link per issue, always paired with something useful: a live coming up, a replay, a free resource. No sales language. Once the paid tier launches, it is pitched to "a cappella directors", never "choir directors".
Start with a hello issueThe list has been quiet for eight months. People who forgot signing up click "spam", and that hurts every later issue. Issue 0 reminds them who it's from and what's coming, with one tip.
Measure clicks and repliesOpen rates are unreliable since Apple Mail started opening emails automatically. We track clicks to Skool, replies, unsubscribes and spam complaints. A "How did you hear about us?" question on the Skool join form, with a "the newsletter" option, shows real conversions.

  1. Subject: the hook, nothing else. No issue numbers.
  2. Opening: 2 or 3 sentences, a moment or a line from Deke.

What has to be built first (one session)

  • Send through Resend Broadcasts rather than the site's own send button. Broadcasts add the one-click unsubscribe header that Gmail and Yahoo now expect, can be scheduled, and report clicks per link. The site's current sender loops one email at a time without that header.
  • Sync the list: the 111 opted-in readers go into a Resend "One Voice" segment, new signups are added automatically, and unsubscribes flow back to the site's database.
  • Turn on click tracking for dekesharon.com (it is off today). Add a tag to every Skool link so we can count them.
  • Restyle the email template: the weekly format above, the new favicon mark in the header, a real postal address in the footer.
  • Engine lane: the newsletter draft step in the Saturday run, plus a Newsletter tab on Deke's review page.
  • Fix the website copy: dekesharon.com/news says "Free. Monthly." and becomes "Free. Every Wednesday."
  • Each issue is archived on dekesharon.com/news (already built), which also helps Google find Deke.
Blocker: a postal address

US law (CAN-SPAM) requires a physical mailing address in every newsletter footer. It can be Deke's business address, a PO box or a virtual mailbox. I could not find one in the project, so you'll need to supply it. Nothing can be sent until we have it.

The Saturday run drafts the issue next to the social week, and the same review page and the same yes cover both. A newsletter-editor agent file holds the rules on this page. Runs on the Max plan with no API costs.

Catch: it pauses whenever the engine pauses. Today the engine is in dry-run mode and the Postiz queue has 3 days left, so that needs fixing too.

A separate weekly routine

A newsletter-only cloud routine with its own review email to Deke. Keeps working if the engine is paused.

Catch: two approvals a week for Deke, and the newsletter and posts can drift onto different themes.
